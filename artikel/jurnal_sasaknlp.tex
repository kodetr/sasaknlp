\begin{abstract}
Bahasa Sasak is an Austronesian regional language spoken by approximately 3 million people across Lombok Island, West Nusa Tenggara (NTB), Indonesia. Despite its vibrant oral tradition and substantial speaker demographic, it remains an underrepresented, low-resource language in computational linguistics due to the scarcity of annotated corpora, pronounced cross-island dialectal variation, and the absence of standardized Natural Language Processing (NLP) toolkits. This paper introduces \textbf{SasakNLP}, the first research-grade, zero-dependency computational framework engineered specifically for Sasak text normalization, reduplication-aware tokenization, shibboleth-driven dialect identification, and dictionary-enhanced multi-candidate morphological disambiguation grounded in the authentic lexicon of Balai Bahasa Provinsi NTB. 

Rigorous empirical evaluation against a 100,000-pair gold-standard morphological benchmark and a 12,591-sentence authentic corpus demonstrates that SasakNLP achieves an overall morphological accuracy of \textbf{87.98\%}, a macro precision of \textbf{99.00\%}, a macro recall of \textbf{95.35\%}, and a macro F1-score of \textbf{96.22\%}. McNemar's statistical significance test confirms a decisive performance advantage over greedy affix-stripping baselines ($\chi^2 = 14,962.14, p < 0.0001$). Error taxonomy analysis reveals near-complete elimination of overstemming (0.00\%) and understemming (0.09\%), alongside resilient handling of Out-Of-Vocabulary items (11.92\%) via non-destructive fallbacks. Computationally, SasakNLP delivers high-throughput processing at \textbf{22,500 words per second} with an average per-token latency of \textbf{0.044 ms} on standard consumer hardware. All code, datasets, benchmark pairs, and interactive demonstrations are publicly released under permissive open-source licenses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{ecent} breakthroughs in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Models (LLMs) have been overwhelmingly concentrated on high-resource languages such as English and Mandarin Chinese \cite{jurafsky2024, magueresse2020}. In Indonesia, while the national language (Bahasa Indonesia) enjoys mature computational infrastructure---such as Sastrawi lemmmatizers \cite{adriani2007, asian2005} and BERT-based representations \cite{aji2022}---more than 700 indigenous regional languages remain critically neglected \cite{aji2022}.

Bahasa Sasak (\textit{Basa Sasak}) is the predominant indigenous language of Lombok Island in West Nusa Tenggara, spoken by approximately 3 million native speakers \cite{teeuw1958, mahsun2006}. Categorized typologically within the Western Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family, Sasak exhibits distinct grammatical characteristics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Complex Morphophonemics}: Stem formation incorporates active prefixes (\texttt{te-}, \texttt{ka-}, \texttt{se-}, \texttt{pe-}), archaic passive infixes (\texttt{-in-}, \texttt{-um-}), causative/applicative suffixes (\texttt{-ang}, \texttt{-an}, \texttt{-i}, \texttt{-in}), iterative circumfixes (\texttt{pe-...-an}, \texttt{te-...-ang}), and intricate nasal substitutions ($N$-).
    \item \textbf{Enclitic Compounding}: Pronominal possessive enclitics (\texttt{-ku}, \texttt{-m}, \texttt{-ne}) and honorific sociolinguistic particles (\texttt{-de}, \texttt{-te}) append directly to root words, frequently triggering severe overstemming or understemming in naive heuristic parsers.
    \item \textbf{Dialectal Diversity}: The language is partitioned into five distinct dialect clusters separated by isoglosses and diagnostic lexical markers (\textit{shibboleths}) \cite{teeuw1958, mahsun2006}.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these barriers, we present \textbf{SasakNLP}, an open-source, reproducible computational toolkit. The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A modular 6-stage NLP architecture combining NFC orthographic normalization, reduplication preservation, dialect classification, and dictionary-validated multi-candidate ranking.
    \item The construction and release of the largest curated Sasak morphological benchmark (100,000 verified pairs) and authentic sentence corpus (12,591 sentences).
    \item Comprehensive empirical benchmarking, error taxonomy breakdown, statistical significance testing, and computational efficiency profiling (22,500 wps, 0.044 ms latency).
    \item Public ecosystem deployment across PyPI, Hugging Face Hub, and interactive web environments \cite{sasaknlp2026, sasakbenchmark2026}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Linguistic and Dialectal Background}

\subsection{Morphological Structure of Bahasa Sasak}
According to official dialectological surveys by Balai Bahasa Provinsi NTB \cite{balaibahasa2017}, the productive affixation inventory of Bahasa Sasak consists of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prefixes}: Passive \texttt{te-} (\textit{tepinaq} ``is made''), stative \texttt{ka-} (\textit{kasolah} ``beautified''), equative \texttt{se-} (\textit{sebale} ``one house''), nominalizer \texttt{pe-} / \texttt{peng-} (\textit{pegawi} ``worker'').
    \item \textbf{Suffixes}: Causative/applicative \texttt{-ang} (\textit{tulungang} ``help for someone''), locative/resultative \texttt{-an} (\textit{kelororan} ``waterway''), iterative \texttt{-i} / \texttt{-in} (\textit{sirami} ``spray repeatedly'').
    \item \textbf{Infixes}: Archaic passive \texttt{-in-} (\textit{tinulung} ``received assistance'') and intransitive \texttt{-um-} (\textit{gumingsir} ``shift'').
    \item \textbf{Circumfixes}: Abstract nominal \texttt{pe-...-an} (\textit{pegawian} ``occupation''), passive applicative \texttt{te-...-ang} (\textit{tetulungang} ``was asked for help''), reciprocal \texttt{be-...-an} (\textit{betulungan} ``help each other'').
    \item \textbf{Enclitics}: First person \texttt{-ku}, second person \texttt{-m}, third person \texttt{-ne}, and polite register markers \texttt{-de}, \texttt{-te} (\textit{baturne} ``his friend'', \textit{balende} ``your house'').
    \item \textbf{Reduplication}: Full reduplication with hyphens (\textit{bareng-bareng} ``together'') and phonetic variations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed System Architecture}

SasakNLP implements a deterministic, multi-stage processing pipeline:

\subsection{Text Normalization and Tokenization}
Raw input strings undergo Unicode NFC composition to eliminate non-standard diacritic encodings. Curved apostrophes (\texttt{’}, \texttt{‘}) and glottal markers are standardized to uniform glottal characters (\texttt{q} or \texttt{'}). Hyphenated reduplications (\textit{bareng-bareng}) are safeguarded against token splitting, preserving grammatical unity.

\subsection{Dialect Shibboleth Detector}
The dialect detector identifies diagnostic vocabulary and computes a weighted confidence metric:
\begin{equation}
\text{Confidence}(d) = \frac{\sum_{w \in T} \mathbb{I}(w \in M_d) \cdot \omega(w)}{\sum_{d' \in D} \sum_{w \in T} \mathbb{I}(w \in M_{d'}) \cdot \omega(w)}
\label{eq:dialect_conf}
\end{equation}
where $T$ represents the tokenized sequence, $M_d$ is the lexical shibboleth set for dialect $d$, and $\omega(w)$ denotes the discriminative weighting factor.

\subsection{Candidate Generation and Trie Validation}
Tokens not found during direct lookup in the primary lexicon are processed by the \texttt{CandidateGenerator}. Affixes are removed in a two-pass sequence: enclitic detachment, confix stripping, prefix/suffix peeling, infix inversion, and reduplication reduction. Generated candidate roots are checked against a high-performance \texttt{PrefixTrie} loaded with the official Balai Bahasa NTB dictionary (2,761 entries) \cite{balaibahasa2017}.

\subsection{Multi-Criteria Candidate Ranking}
When multiple candidates satisfy grammatical stripping rules, disambiguation is resolved through a multi-criteria scoring objective:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Score}(c) = & \; w_{\text{lex}} \cdot S_{\text{lex}}(c) + w_{\text{morph}} \cdot S_{\text{morph}}(c) \\
& + w_{\text{conf}} \cdot S_{\text{conf}}(c) + w_{\text{freq}} \cdot S_{\text{freq}}(c) \\
& + w_{\text{dial}} \cdot S_{\text{dial}}(c)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ranking_score}
\end{equation}
with empirical default parameters $w_{\text{lex}} = 0.40$, $w_{\text{morph}} = 0.25$, $w_{\text{conf}} = 0.15$, $w_{\text{freq}} = 0.10$, and $w_{\text{dial}} = 0.10$.

\section{Dataset Acquisition and Quality Protocol}
As illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:dataset_pipeline}, the empirical corpus was developed via a 3-phase scientific protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Ingestion}: Gathering authentic textual materials from traditional oral folklore (\textit{Putri Mandalika}, \textit{Dewi Anjani}), regional cultural texts, and the official Balai Bahasa NTB dictionary.
    \item \textbf{Five-Stage Curation}: Implementing diacritic restoration, removal of foreign non-indigenous proper names, pseudo-root eradication, dictionary notation deconvolution, and stratified sampling.
    \item \textbf{Gold-Standard Generation}: Producing the 100,000-pair benchmark (\texttt{benchmark\_100k.csv}), the 12,591-sentence authentic corpus (\texttt{sasak\_sentences\_large.csv}), and the 2,761-entry lexicon (\texttt{kamus\_balai\_bahasa\_ntb.csv}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical Evaluation and Results}

\subsection{Baseline Comparison and Statistical Significance}
SasakNLP was compared against two classical baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline 1 (Direct Lexicon Lookup)}: Verbatim matching against the 2,761-word dictionary without morphological rules.
    \item \textbf{Baseline 2 (Greedy Affix Stripping)}: Longest-match rule-based stripping without dictionary validation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Downstream Impact: Feature Space Compression}
In extrinsic evaluation, applying SasakNLP lemmatization compressed the vocabulary feature space of the 189k-word authentic corpus from 5,913 unique surface tokens to 4,022 base lemmas (a 31.98\% dimensional compression), directly reducing sparsity in downstream vector space models and sentiment classifiers.

\section{Conclusion and Future Directions}
This paper presented \textbf{SasakNLP}, an open-source, research-grade morphological processing and dialect-aware toolkit for Bahasa Sasak. Benchmarked on 100,000 verified pairs, SasakNLP achieves an 87.98\% accuracy rate, exceptional 99.00\% precision, near-zero overstemming, and sub-millisecond execution latency. Future work will investigate neural sequence-to-sequence morphological parsing, Sasak dependency treebanks, and bidirectional neural machine translation.
